# Prototype boundaries

This is a complete playable exhibition prototype, not a production football release or an official-law simulator. The verification report distinguishes implemented features from measured evidence.

- **Art:** original stylised procedural bodies and stadium, with an original static SVG title illustration rather than a photographed or fully rendered title scene. Approximate two-bone leg IK and stance foot plants reduce sliding on the flat pitch, but do not provide exact physical contact or terrain adaptation. No motion capture, face rig, hair strands, cloth or photorealistic crowd. Ball rendering is enlarged for readability relative to its physical 0.11 m radius. Kick poses are responsive follow-through approximations, not exact physics-driven kicks. Goal replays use a short visual snapshot buffer and a fixed Telebroadcast view; there is no replay editor, free replay camera or video export.
- **Rules:** simplified receiving-involvement offside with a visible restart reason; no passive interference or comprehensive deliberate-play interpretation. Assisted, automatically executed restarts; throw-ins use an assisted pass redistribution without an overhead throw animation. Standing and sliding challenges compare geometric contact with the ball and opponents, awarding a foul for opponent contact before the ball. This uses simplified ground footprints, not full-body contact physics or a complete dangerous-play model. No handball, cards, advantage, extra time, shoot-outs, substitutions, injuries or detailed referee logic. Draws are final.
- **Gameplay:** timed headers, volleys and clearances use bounded swept contact and a short action window, without full-body aerial collision, pushing or dangerous-play adjudication. There is no advanced aerial-duel system, skill-move catalogue or manual keeper diving. Dribbling and player/ball collisions are assisted. Charged passes use assisted receiver selection and bounded lead, not unrestricted manual placement. Normal, finesse and chip shots share charge and assisted goal targeting. Finesse uses bounded airborne lateral acceleration; chips use a slower lob, with long attempts falling short. Neither uses full aerodynamic spin physics. Shot and pass guides show intended aim and power; dispersion, defenders and the goalkeeper still affect the result. Keeper position forecasts and deterministic swept contact are simplified, ability-scaled gameplay decisions rather than anatomical reach or complete goalkeeper tactics. Third person is an elevated follow/orbit camera rather than an eye-level view. AI makes useful formation and pressing decisions but needs extensive balance/playtesting. Match difficulty does not change physical speed limits. No equal-strength win-rate claim from a handful of seeded matches.
- **Practice:** two repeatable offline drills cover shooting/aim and slide-contact timing. There is no passing course, tutorial curriculum, online training, persistent drill history or progression. Attempts and successes last only for the current practice session; restart clears them.
- **Networking:** in-memory rooms on one persistent process; no accounts or lobby discovery. Reconnect works within the same page, not after reload/token loss. Public WSS hosting, geographically distant play and server load capacity remain unverified. Visual movement prediction is bounded and reconciled, without full rollback; jitter may cause visible corrections. Server restart destroys rooms. A static deployment supports offline play; a separate WSS service is required for human-versus-human play. The hosted client supports offline play; public multiplayer hosting is not included.
- **Devices:** keyboard and emulated standard controller/touch can be tested in the browser. Physical controller, Android/iOS, Bluetooth latency, mobile thermal performance and Safari behaviour require physical-device testing. Touch action buttons, the joystick and shot selector can be repositioned and resized, with separate landscape/portrait saves. A smaller viewport uses a safe default if saved controls would overlap. Controller-driven menus and user key remapping are deferred.
- **Performance:** 60 fps desktop and 30 fps physical mobile are targets. The measured Intel Iris Plus desktop result on Medium is below 60 fps. Automatic adjustment samples sustained active-play frame times, lowers detail then render resolution, and restores quality with hysteresis. Menus/background gaps are excluded. Manual graphics remain a ceiling; automatic changes do not overwrite the saved choice. Lower settings reduce render resolution, shadows and spectators without reducing player count. Resolution scaling cannot eliminate CPU or thermal bottlenecks. No physical mobile benchmark is claimed.
- **Sound:** original synthesized contact effects, whistle patterns and collective gasp, plus four edited CC BY 4.0 stadium ambience/cheering excerpts. Camera-relative stereo and approximate distance attenuation, with bounded voices and a compressed/limited mix. Crowd intensity uses ball position and match events, not a detailed supporter simulation. No commentary, music, licensed club chants, HRTF/occlusion or room acoustics. Mobile speaker/headphone balance, Bluetooth latency and physical Safari/iPhone audio interruptions still need device listening tests.
- **Accessibility:** semantic menus, contrast, shape cues, mute, camera settings and visible controls are provided. Football gameplay is not fully accessible to non-visual players.

Intentionally excluded: career mode, packs, transfers, tournaments, accounts, purchases and persistent progression. The next work should improve football feel and device/network confidence before adding scope.
